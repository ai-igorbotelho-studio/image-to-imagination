## 1. The parent vision: Active Transition

**Active Transition** is the book and worldview of **Igor Botelho, Founder**, and the parent brand
of four practice areas. They are not four independent businesses: they are **four expressions of the
same method** applied to different domains.

**Tension → Turn → Transformation**
- **Tension:** organizations promise sustainability and a future on the surface (campaign, label,
  report) without structural substance. This is **Green Washing**.
- **Turn:** Active Transition is **a method before it is a studio**. Every engagement delivers both
  a **tangible quick win** and a **long-term regenerative architecture**.
- **Transformation:** the organization leaves with a real, measurable **Green Legacy**, not a campaign.

**Parent tagline:** "Active Transition: design the future."

**Brand architecture: endorsed.** The parent brand lends intellectual authority (book, worldview,
method); each area carries its own tactical specialty and can be sold on its own.

```
ACTIVE TRANSITION (parent brand — worldview / book / method)
 ├─ Transition Brands    — brand identity and positioning       · "design the identity"
 ├─ Transition Cultures  — culture, communication, digital presence · "design the voice"
 ├─ Transition Futures   — boutique trend hunting and futurism  · "design what's next"
 └─ Transition Journeys  — experience travel                    · "design the experience"
```

Exact endorsement line (e.g. "Transition Brands, an Active Transition practice"): **[open]**.

## 2. Transition Brands — the operating area

**Specialty:** creating and managing brands that point to viable futures: naming, brand
architecture, verbal and visual identity, positioning.

**Ambition:** professional, high-end, exceptionally creative, business-driven, global. Quality
benchmark: Pentagram, Wolff Olins, COLLINS, Koto, Landor, Porto Rocha, Jones Knowles Ritchie,
Studio Dumbar, Chermayeff & Geismar & Haviv, Mucho, Experimental Jetset, &Walsh, DixonBaxi,
Ragged Edge, Pearlfisher, Interbrand, Siegel+Gale. **A benchmark, never a reference to copy.**

**Area tagline:** "design the identity".

### The two-speed model (applies to every engagement)

| Mode | What it is | At Transition Brands | Horizon |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Proof Sprint** | Fast, highly visible tactical delivery: proof that the transition is real | Brand audit + tactical repositioning of **one** critical touchpoint (manifesto, positioning line, first applied piece of visual identity) | Weeks |
| **Regenerative Architecture** | Structural, long-term build that sustains the Sprint's result | Complete brand architecture: hierarchy, versioned brand book, governance system, designed to last generations | Long term |

The Proof Sprint is **not a cheap version** of the Architecture: it is its first stone. Everything the
Sprint produces must be coherent with the future architecture. Official commercial name for this
methodology: **[open]**. (The Portuguese source terms are "Sprint de Prova" and "Arquitetura
Regenerativa".)

## 3. Audience

**Core**
- **Family Offices** (and related family businesses and foundations).
- **Not-for-Profit organizations:** NGOs, institutes, foundations.

**Secondary** (entered through core social proof or one-off projects): multinationals, startups,
family businesses, brands in rebranding, governments, universities.

**Insight that unites the core:** both must **prove real impact to skeptical stakeholders** (heirs
and beneficiaries on one side; donors and boards on the other), and both decide on a **longer horizon**
than traditional companies.

### What this means for the work
- **Family Offices:** discretion and reputation over visibility. Many family brands speak to a few
  audiences (family, next generation, co-investors, managers, philanthropic grantees), not to a mass
  market. Recurring themes: succession, next generation, legacy, family governance, philanthropic
  arm, impact investing. Confidentiality is a given.
- **Not-for-Profits:** donors and boards question impact; the brand must connect cause, theory of
  change and evidence. Recurring themes: fundraising, trust, accountability, relationships with
  beneficiaries and communities, limited resources (price and scope must respect this without lowering
  the standard).
- **Secondary:** same rigor; the guiding question remains "can the organization sustain what the brand
  will promise?"

## 5. Tone of voice

**Sounds like:** "Your brand already has a legacy. The question is whether it points to the right future."
**Does not sound like:** "We help you become more sustainable and innovative." (jargon without proof —
exactly the Green Washing the brand exists to correct)

Shared DNA, area accent. **Transition Brands = strategic-corporate, consulting-grade rigor.**
(Cultures: conversational-digital · Futures: editorial-provocative · Journeys: sensory, first person.)

**Practical rules for all studio and client communication**
- Every claim comes with proof, mechanism or data.
- Banned as empty adjectives: sustainable, innovative, disruptive, eco, green, conscious, purpose,
  impact (unless you say which impact and how it is measured).
- Short sentences, concrete verbs, conclusion first.

## 7. Business success metric

**Conversion rate**, in two layers:
- Proposal conversion by segment (Family Office · NFP · secondary).
- First conversation → proposal conversion.

A low first layer with a high second layer suggests a proposal or pricing problem; a low second layer
suggests an audience or messaging problem.
