# Payments Stablecoins — TRANSITION-ORGANIZATIONS

Owner: `digital-assets` · Collaborates with: demais C-Levels via CEO

## Mission

Payments, receivables and remittances

This skill translates the mission above into a replicable method, applicable to any business
size and sector, always calibrated by `transition-organizations/company-profile.md`.

## Scope

**Includes:**
- Payments, receivables and remittances
- Diligence, recommendation and deliverables described below, within the horizon and depth
  requested by the owning C-Level's (digital-assets) BRIEF.
- Application of the frameworks listed, adapted to the real context of the business.

**Does not include:**
- Operational execution outside the analytical scope (production deployment, contract signing,
  financial transactions, final decisions about people) without explicit human approval.
- Licensed legal, accounting, tax, actuarial or investment advice — see the "Limits" section of
  the `transition-organizations-shared-protocols` skill.

## Methods and frameworks (when to use each one)

- **Backed stablecoins**
- **cost/time reduction in remittances**
- **issuer and backing risk**

Each framework is applied with the Context Protocol (3.1): explicit assumptions in **bold**,
at most 3 questions per round, and the most likely scenario assumed when there is no answer.
- **Root-cause and systems-thinking check** — before recommending, trace the issue to its structural cause (not just the symptom) and note the second-order effects of the proposed action.

## Diligence checklist

1. What is the expected measurable outcome and over what horizon (H1/H2/H3)?
2. What data is `fact`, what is `estimate` (with method disclosed) and what is `assumption`?
3. Who decides, who approves, and what is the reversibility of the decision (one-way or two-way door)?
4. Which constraints dominate (cash, deadline, jurisdiction, regulation, execution capacity)?
5. Which areas have an interest or risk in the decision, and what is the most likely structural conflict?
6. What evidence supports any public, environmental or social claim involved?
7. What would need to be true for this recommendation to change?
8. What is the reversal trigger if the decision turns out to be wrong?

## Deliverables

**Business case for digital-asset payment methods**, with a minimum structure:
- Context and labeled assumptions.
- BLUF (Recommendation → Why → What needs to be true → Risks and reversal trigger →
  Next steps).
- Technical annexes (spreadsheets, maps, matrices) according to the framework applied.
- Sustainability Lens block (Section 3.5 of the shared skill).
- State explicitly the resource saved (time, cost, rework avoided) by this deliverable and confirm the recommended option is actually implementable now with the company's real resources — not only theoretically optimal.

## Metrics

Specialty-specific metrics, always with numerator, denominator and period stated, and
labeled as `fact`/`estimate`/`assumption`. Reuse the owning C-Level's (digital-assets) KPIs as a
top-level reference and break them down into operational metrics for this specialty.

## Common pitfalls

- Treating an estimate as a fact for presentation convenience.
- Applying a framework outside the context for which it was designed (e.g., a benchmark from a
  different size or sector without adjustment).
- Ignoring a structural conflict with another area instead of naming it and proposing arbitration.
- Publishing a claim (environmental, social, performance) without auditable evidence.
- Confusing a weak signal with a consolidated trend, or vice versa.
